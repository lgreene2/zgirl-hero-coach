## 4. Licensed Rights

Subject to the final agreement, Licensor proposes to grant Licensee a limited, non-exclusive, non-transferable, non-sublicensable right during the license term to:

- use the approved Z-Girl institutional implementation package;
- provide the approved participant experience to the defined audience;
- use approved facilitator / coach materials internally;
- use approved family communication materials;
- access approved updates released for the licensed profile during the term; and
- use Z-Girl branding only as expressly authorized for the licensed implementation.

No ownership of Z-Girl intellectual property transfers to Licensee.

---

## 5. Excluded Rights

Unless separately authorized in writing, the proposed license does **not** permit Licensee to:

- resell Z-Girl products or access;
- sublicense Z-Girl to another organization;
- publish protected resources publicly;
- create a commercial derivative product from Z-Girl materials;
- train unaffiliated organizations using Z-Girl materials;
- represent itself as a certified Z-Girl trainer;
- create its own Z-Girl certification;
- remove copyright, trademark, brand, or attribution notices;
- use Z-Girl characters, media, or merchandise rights outside the approved institutional package; or
- access private participant reflections as institutional reporting data.

## 7. Privacy and Institutional Data Boundary

The annual license should preserve the following non-negotiable product boundary:

**Private participant reflection text is not institutional reporting data.**

The institutional reporting model may use approved de-identified or aggregate implementation measures, including:

- participation;
- completion;
- usefulness;
- return / reuse;
- confidence / self-efficacy proxy;
- Hero Move / action identification;
- accessibility experience;
- safety / respect experience; and
- facilitator implementation quality.

Licensee does not receive a student, athlete, youth, or participant private-reflection dashboard under the standard institutional license.

Any proposed change to the data model requires separate written approval and legal / privacy review before implementation.

---

## 8. Safeguarding and Professional Boundaries

Z-Girl is not therapy, medical care, diagnosis, treatment, emergency support, or a substitute for qualified professional services.

Licensee remains responsible for its own:

- safeguarding policies;
- mandatory-reporting obligations;
- crisis and emergency procedures;
- supervision;
- staffing;
- parental / caregiver notices and permissions it is responsible for obtaining;
- accommodations it is legally required to provide; and
- access to qualified professional or emergency support.

Facilitators may support the reflection process but should not pressure disclosure or use the product to diagnose participants.

## 13. Train-the-Trainer Option

Train-the-Trainer is a separate expansion right and should not be implied by the standard annual license.

A future train-the-trainer addendum should define:

- eligibility;
- trainer prerequisites;
- training hours;
- assessment requirements;
- observed practice;
- permitted internal training scope;
- certification / designation language, if any;
- renewal requirements;
- quality assurance;
- continuing education;
- revocation / suspension standards; and
- trainer branding rights.

Until that system is formally released, organizations may describe staff only as approved facilitators or coaches within the licensed implementation — not certified Z-Girl trainers.

## 14. Updates and Version Governance

Licensor may improve the Z-Girl system during the term. Material changes affecting the approved institutional profile, privacy boundary, participant workflow, or implementation responsibilities should be communicated to Licensee.

Specialized profiles should remain versioned and governed rather than silently changing the core system.

Licensee-specific customizations should be documented so they can be distinguished from core Z-Girl releases.

## 16. Security, Privacy, and Legal Review

Before final execution, the Parties should review the implementation against applicable requirements for the specific setting and participant population.

For school implementations, procurement may require a separate student-data privacy or data-processing addendum even where Z-Girl intentionally minimizes the data it receives.

For implementations involving children under 13, the Parties should specifically review age, consent, online data collection, privacy notice, retention, deletion, and vendor / school responsibilities before launch.

The standard product design should continue to minimize the need for transfer of education records, medical records, counseling records, diagnosis information, or private participant reflection text.
