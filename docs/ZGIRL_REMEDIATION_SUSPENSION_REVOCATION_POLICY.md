# Z-Girl Remediation, Suspension & Revocation Policy

Version: 1.0

## Purpose

Define a fair, documented response when a candidate or credential holder does not meet Z-Girl authorization, conduct, safety, privacy, implementation, or license standards.

## Guiding principles

- protect participants first;
- distinguish coaching needs from critical violations;
- use the least restrictive response consistent with safety, trust, and program integrity;
- document decisions and evidence;
- avoid using private participant reflections as routine quality evidence;
- provide a reasonable remediation/appeal route unless immediate action is required.

## Issue categories

### Category A — Developmental / Non-critical

Examples:
- inconsistent pacing;
- weak session transitions;
- unclear but correctable explanation of aggregate measures;
- minor profile-fidelity drift that does not affect safety/privacy;
- accessibility implementation gap that can be corrected without material harm.

Typical response:
- coaching;
- targeted refresher;
- supervised session;
- reassessment.

### Category B — Material Quality / Boundary Concern

Examples:
- repeated pressure to share despite coaching;
- persistent role confusion;
- use of outdated governed materials after notice;
- repeated inaccurate credential representation;
- repeated failure to complete required implementation records;
- trainer scoring that remains materially uncalibrated.

Typical response:
- Active — Conditions;
- temporary restriction;
- formal remediation plan;
- observed reassessment;
- possible suspension if unresolved.

### Category C — Critical

Examples:
- forced private disclosure;
- routine sharing of identifiable private reflection content;
- diagnosis/treatment/clinical scoring outside role;
- presenting Z-Girl as emergency, clinical, medical, clergy/spiritual-direction, or sports-medicine service;
- ignoring a required safeguarding/emergency escalation;
- promising secrecy in conflict with law/institutional duty;
- intentional credential fraud;
- unauthorized training/credential issuance;
- intentional circumvention of suspension/revocation;
- material misuse of Z-Girl IP creating participant or brand risk.

Typical response:
- immediate suspension pending review;
- removal from delivery/training role;
- formal investigation through appropriate institutional/Z-Girl process;
- revocation where substantiated and proportionate.

## Suspension

Suspension means the credential is not active for the restricted role during the suspension period.

During suspension the holder may not:
- represent the credential as active;
- independently facilitate where the credential is required;
- train candidates under Level 3 rights;
- use active-status badges/records.

Suspension notice should state:
- effective date;
- scope;
- reason category;
- interim restrictions;
- remediation/review conditions;
- appeal route;
- next review date where appropriate.

## Revocation

Revocation ends the credential before normal expiration.

Revocation may be appropriate for:
- substantiated critical violation;
- intentional credential fraud;
- repeated material violations after remediation;
- unauthorized credential issuance/training with material risk;
- refusal to comply with required corrective action;
- conduct incompatible with participant safety or program integrity.

Revocation record should contain:
- decision date;
- scope;
- policy basis;
- evidence summary;
- reviewer/authority;
- appeal status;
- reapplication eligibility, if any.

## Reapplication after revocation

Not automatic.

The credential authority may specify:
- no reapplication;
- waiting period;
- full retraining;
- enhanced practicum;
- institutional approval;
- additional conduct conditions.

## Immediate safety action

Nothing in this policy delays an institution's lawful safeguarding, emergency, employment, or reporting duties.

If immediate risk exists, the institution should act under its applicable process first; credential review can follow.

## Appeals

Unless immediate safety/legal constraints require otherwise, the holder may appeal within 15 calendar days.

An appeal does not automatically lift a suspension.

## Institutional notification

Where the credential is tied to an institutional license, Z-Girl may notify the authorized institutional contact of status changes necessary to administer the license. Notification should be limited to credential/implementation information and should not include unnecessary private participant content.

## Trend review

Credential authority should periodically review de-identified/administrative trends in:
- common remediation domains;
- assessment failures;
- critical incidents;
- trainer calibration drift;
- version-compliance issues;
- renewal lapses.

Use trends to improve training and standards without creating a participant-surveillance dataset.
