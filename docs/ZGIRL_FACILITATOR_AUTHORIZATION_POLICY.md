# Z-Girl Facilitator Authorization Policy

Version: 1.0

## Purpose

This policy defines how Z-Girl program authorization is granted, scoped, documented, renewed, suspended, lapsed, or revoked.

## Authorization is not certification or licensure

Z-Girl authorization confirms that an individual has met current Z-Girl internal program standards for a defined role and scope. It does not create professional licensure, government accreditation, academic credit, clinical qualification, or authority outside the person's lawful employment/professional role.

Approved public titles:
- Z-Girl Authorized Facilitator
- Z-Girl Authorized Lead Facilitator
- Z-Girl Institutional Trainer — Authorized

Do not use “Certified Z-Girl Counselor,” “Licensed Z-Girl Practitioner,” or similar professionalized claims.

## Authorization levels

### Level 0 — Orientation Complete
Not an authorization credential.

### Level 1 — Authorized Facilitator
May independently deliver approved Z-Girl sessions within the credential scope and applicable institutional license.

### Level 2 — Authorized Lead Facilitator
May lead implementation quality, mentor/co-facilitate, and support supervised candidate practice within the licensed institution. May not independently grant Z-Girl credentials.

### Level 3 — Institutional Trainer Authorization
May deliver approved Z-Girl facilitator training inside the licensed institutional scope and submit candidate evidence/recommendations. Final credential authority remains with Z-Girl unless an agreement explicitly delegates a defined administrative function.

## Required initial evidence — Level 1

A complete authorization file contains:
1. candidate identity/contact details appropriate to administration;
2. institution/role and approved implementation scope;
3. core training completion record;
4. local safeguarding/emergency orientation acknowledgment;
5. knowledge-assessment result meeting standard;
6. practicum result meeting standard;
7. facilitator conduct/privacy/IP agreement;
8. current version acknowledgment;
9. authorization decision record;
10. credential ID, issue date, expiration date, tier, scope, and status.

No private participant reflection text should be included as routine credential evidence.

## Initial pass standard

Knowledge:
- at least 22/25 correct; and
- every designated critical item correct.

Practicum:
- at least 40/48; and
- every critical domain at least 2/3; and
- no critical-fail behavior.

All administrative/acknowledgment requirements must also be complete.

## Decision outcomes

### Authorized
All standards met. Credential becomes Active.

### Authorized — Conditions
Use only when the issue is non-critical and the candidate has met the core pass standard. Conditions must be written, time-limited, and monitored.

Examples:
- first live session co-facilitated;
- targeted accessibility coaching;
- additional profile-specific orientation.

### Remediation Required
Candidate has not met one or more authorization standards but the concern is remediable. Candidate remains unauthorized until reassessed.

### Do Not Authorize
Used when:
- a critical-fail behavior is unresolved;
- candidate refuses required boundaries/conduct terms;
- evidence integrity is compromised;
- material risk remains after remediation;
- credential authority determines the candidate is not suitable for the authorized role.

## Scope fields

Every active credential should define at minimum:
- credential ID;
- holder name;
- tier;
- status;
- issue date;
- expiration date;
- approved institution/network if restricted;
- approved profile(s): Core / EDU / Faith & Values / Athlete / other approved profile;
- delivery role;
- version requirement;
- any conditions.

## Credential IDs

Recommended format:
`ZG-AF-YYYY-#####` for Authorized Facilitator  
`ZG-AL-YYYY-#####` for Authorized Lead Facilitator  
`ZG-IT-YYYY-#####` for Institutional Trainer

IDs should not encode private participant information.

## Credential status

- Pending
- Active
- Active — Conditions
- Expiring
- Lapsed
- Suspended
- Revoked
- Retired

## Term

Default authorization term: 12 months.

A shorter term may be used for:
- pilot-specific authorization;
- conditional authorization;
- corrective action;
- temporary institutional assignment.

## Level 2 advancement

Minimum evidence:
- active Level 1 credential;
- two completed implementation cycles or approved equivalent evidence;
- satisfactory aggregate implementation quality;
- no unresolved critical issue;
- observed lead session meeting Level 2 rubric expectations;
- coaching/calibration review;
- credential authority approval.

## Level 3 advancement

Minimum evidence:
- active Level 2 credential;
- trainer curriculum completion;
- trainer knowledge assessment;
- teach-back demonstration;
- practicum-scoring calibration;
- trainer conduct/license agreement;
- active institutional trainer license;
- credential authority approval.

## Use of credential

Credential holders may:
- accurately state their active authorization title;
- display an approved credential badge/record if issued;
- deliver only within the active scope.

Credential holders may not:
- imply professional licensure or third-party certification;
- use an expired/suspended/revoked credential as active;
- sublicense Z-Girl IP;
- train or authorize others without applicable Level 3 and institutional license;
- create unofficial credential badges/certificates that imply expanded authority;
- modify the credential title.

## Authorization record language

Recommended:

> This record confirms that [Name] has met the current Z-Girl program requirements for [Authorization Tier] within the stated scope and term. This program authorization is not professional licensure, academic accreditation, or clinical certification.

## Institutional dependency

An individual credential does not itself create institutional usage rights. Delivery must also occur under an applicable approved Z-Girl implementation/license where required.

Likewise, an institutional license does not automatically authorize every staff member to represent themselves as Z-Girl Authorized Facilitators.

## Appeals

A candidate/holder may request review of an authorization, suspension, or revocation decision within 15 calendar days of notice unless a contract or safety process requires a different timeframe.

Appeals should:
- identify the decision being challenged;
- state the factual basis;
- include non-private credential evidence;
- avoid attaching participant private reflection text unless specifically required through a separate lawful process.

The appeal reviewer should not be the sole original decision-maker when a reasonable alternate reviewer is available.
