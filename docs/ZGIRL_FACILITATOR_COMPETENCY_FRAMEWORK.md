# Z-Girl Facilitator Competency Framework

Version: 1.0

## Purpose

This framework defines the observable competencies required for Z-Girl program authorization. It is used for training design, candidate self-assessment, knowledge checks, practicum observation, remediation, renewal, and institutional quality assurance.

## Rating scale

- **0 — Unsafe / Not Demonstrated:** behavior conflicts with a critical requirement or cannot be demonstrated.
- **1 — Emerging:** partial understanding; needs direct support before independent delivery.
- **2 — Competent:** performs the requirement consistently with normal reference materials.
- **3 — Strong / Transferable:** performs consistently, explains the reason behind the standard, and adapts appropriately without breaking governance boundaries.

## Competency Domain 1 — Purpose & Reflection Model

The facilitator can:
- explain Z-Girl as a guided reflection and encouragement system;
- describe the core flow: pause, name what is happening, notice strengths/support, choose a practical next step / Hero Move;
- distinguish guided reflection from counseling, therapy, diagnosis, treatment, clergy/spiritual direction, sports medicine, or emergency response;
- explain why private reflection ownership matters;
- avoid turning the session into advice-giving, interrogation, evaluation, or forced disclosure.

Critical standard: accurately describes Z-Girl's role limitations.

## Domain 2 — Participant Choice & Psychological Safety

The facilitator can:
- offer pause, skip, stop, and trusted-person options without punishment or stigma;
- avoid coercive sharing;
- normalize different levels of participation;
- use developmentally appropriate, non-shaming language;
- respond to silence or reluctance without pressure;
- preserve a clear route to a trusted adult or local support structure.

Critical standard: never requires a participant to reveal private reflection content.

## Domain 3 — Privacy & Data Minimization

The facilitator can:
- explain what is and is not collected for routine implementation;
- keep private reflection text outside institutional scorecards and routine facilitator reporting;
- distinguish implementation data from reflection content;
- avoid unnecessary collection of diagnosis, counseling, medical, family, or safeguarding details;
- use only approved aggregate/de-identified reporting fields;
- describe the limits of confidentiality appropriately.

Critical standard: no participant-identifiable private reflection content in routine institutional reporting.

## Domain 4 — Safeguarding, Distress & Escalation

The facilitator can:
- recognize when a concern leaves the normal reflection workflow;
- follow the institution's approved safeguarding/emergency/escalation process;
- avoid attempting to investigate, diagnose, treat, or independently manage a crisis;
- avoid promises of secrecy that conflict with law or institutional duty;
- explain the difference between Z-Girl support boundaries and local professional/support responsibilities;
- document only what the institution's lawful process requires, outside routine reflection reporting.

Critical standard: follows required escalation and does not substitute Z-Girl for qualified support.

## Domain 5 — Accessibility & Inclusive Facilitation

The facilitator can:
- offer approved choices in pace, modality, reading support, response mode, sensory load, and participation format;
- avoid requiring diagnosis disclosure as a standard condition for support;
- use plain, respectful language;
- anticipate barriers before sessions;
- coordinate appropriate accommodations with the institution;
- preserve dignity and equivalent participation opportunities.

## Domain 6 — Profile Fidelity

The facilitator can:
- identify the approved profile/version being used;
- deliver only the approved EDU, Faith & Values, Athlete, or other governed content for the implementation;
- distinguish optional profile language from the non-negotiable core boundaries;
- avoid unauthorized theological, clinical, athletic-performance, disciplinary, or diagnostic claims;
- route requested material changes through the approved governance process.

Critical standard: does not materially alter privacy/safety boundaries or claim unapproved expertise.

## Domain 7 — Session Facilitation

The facilitator can:
- open a session with purpose, choice, privacy, and support boundaries;
- guide reflection without dominating the participant's meaning-making;
- manage time and transitions;
- redirect inappropriate group pressure or disclosure requests;
- close with an achievable Hero Move / next-step orientation where appropriate;
- end with support and trusted-person reminders when appropriate.

## Domain 8 — Family & Institutional Communication

The facilitator can:
- explain the pilot/program in language aligned with approved family notices;
- avoid overstating outcomes or making clinical/therapeutic claims;
- explain what the institution receives in aggregate;
- route policy, consent, legal, safeguarding, and records questions to the correct institutional owner;
- preserve commercial/nonprofit boundaries where relevant.

## Domain 9 — Aggregate Evidence & Implementation Quality

The facilitator can:
- use approved measures such as participation, usefulness, accessibility experience, respect/safety experience, Hero Move identification, and implementation fidelity;
- avoid mining private reflections for outcome evidence;
- identify implementation barriers without exposing participant content;
- support accurate closeout and renewal decisions;
- distinguish program-improvement evidence from research claims.

## Domain 10 — Professional Conduct & Brand Stewardship

The facilitator can:
- accurately represent their authorization level and expiration status;
- use current approved materials;
- avoid credential inflation, sublicensing, or unauthorized training of others;
- disclose conflicts or material implementation issues;
- accept coaching, remediation, audit, and version updates;
- protect Z-Girl intellectual property and participant trust.

Critical standard: does not represent orientation, employment, or informal experience as Z-Girl authorization.

## Tier expectations

### Authorized Facilitator

Minimum expected level:
- Competent (2) across all domains;
- no critical-domain rating below 2;
- practicum average at least 2.5/3;
- no critical fail.

### Authorized Lead Facilitator

Expected level:
- Strong / Transferable (3) in Domains 1–4, 7, 9, and 10;
- at least Competent (2) elsewhere;
- demonstrates coaching without granting credentials;
- demonstrates implementation-level judgment.

### Institutional Trainer Authorization

Expected level:
- Strong / Transferable (3) across all critical domains;
- demonstrates accurate rubric scoring and calibration;
- can teach the rationale for privacy/safety boundaries;
- can remediate performance without lowering standards;
- can maintain version and credential records under the licensed process.

## Evidence sources

Competency may be demonstrated through:
- knowledge assessment;
- scenario responses;
- observed practicum;
- live implementation observation;
- aggregate implementation evidence;
- calibration exercise;
- remediation reassessment;
- renewal evidence.

Private participant reflection text is not a required competency-evidence source.
