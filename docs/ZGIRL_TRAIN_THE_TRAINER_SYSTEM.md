# Z-Girl Train-the-Trainer & Facilitator Authorization System

Version: 1.0 operating framework for Z-Girl v2.7

## Purpose

The Z-Girl Train-the-Trainer & Facilitator Authorization System creates a controlled pathway for institutions to prepare people to facilitate Z-Girl consistently while preserving privacy, participant choice, safeguarding boundaries, accessibility, implementation quality, and brand integrity.

This system is designed to support repeatable institutional delivery without converting Z-Girl into an uncontrolled workshop script, private-reflection surveillance tool, clinical service, or unrestricted sublicense.

## Credential boundary

Z-Girl may issue a **program authorization credential** showing that a person has met Z-Girl's current internal standards for an approved role.

That authorization is **not**:

- professional licensure;
- an academic degree;
- a clinical, counseling, medical, pastoral, sports-medicine, or safeguarding qualification;
- government accreditation;
- third-party professional certification; or
- permission to practice outside the individual's existing lawful role.

Public language should use **Authorized Facilitator**, **Authorized Lead Facilitator**, or **Institutional Trainer Authorization** unless and until a separately governed certification system is formally established.

## Operating principles

1. **The reflection belongs to the participant.** Authorization does not grant access to private reflection text.
2. **Facilitation is not therapy.** Facilitators do not diagnose, clinically score, treat, or interpret private reflections as health records.
3. **Choice is preserved.** Participants retain pause, skip, stop, and trusted-person pathways.
4. **Safeguarding stays local and lawful.** Institutions retain emergency, mandated-reporting, safeguarding, consent/permission, records, and duty-of-care responsibilities.
5. **Accessibility is operational, not diagnostic.** Facilitators provide approved access supports without requiring diagnosis disclosure to Z-Girl as a standard condition.
6. **Profiles are governed.** EDU, Faith & Values, Athlete, and other specialized profiles may be used only within their approved scope/version.
7. **Authorization is renewable and revocable.** Status depends on continued competence, conduct, version currency, and license status.
8. **Institutional training rights are licensed.** An authorized individual does not automatically gain the right to train others.

## Authorization ladder

### Level 0 — Orientation Complete

Status: **Not authorized to independently facilitate.**

Evidence:
- completed orientation or introductory training;
- acknowledged role/privacy/safety boundaries.

Permitted:
- observe an approved session;
- participate in supervised practice;
- assist with logistics within institutional policy.

Not permitted:
- independently represent themselves as a Z-Girl Authorized Facilitator;
- independently deliver a Z-Girl institutional implementation;
- train other facilitators.

### Level 1 — Z-Girl Authorized Facilitator

Purpose: deliver approved Z-Girl sessions for the audience/profile and institution named in the authorization scope.

Initial requirements:
- complete all core facilitator modules;
- pass the knowledge assessment;
- pass observed practicum;
- complete facilitator agreement and conduct acknowledgment;
- demonstrate current safeguarding/escalation knowledge for the local institution;
- receive an active credential ID and expiration date.

### Level 2 — Z-Girl Authorized Lead Facilitator

Purpose: lead implementation quality for an approved site/cohort and coach Level 0/Level 1 staff inside the licensed implementation without independently granting credentials.

Additional requirements:
- active Level 1 authorization;
- at least two completed Z-Girl implementation cycles or equivalent approved evidence;
- no unresolved critical conduct or quality issue;
- satisfactory aggregate implementation evidence;
- lead-facilitator calibration review;
- successful observed lead session;
- approval by the Z-Girl credential authority.

### Level 3 — Institutional Trainer Authorization

Purpose: deliver approved Z-Girl facilitator training inside a specifically licensed institution/network and submit candidates for Z-Girl authorization decisions.

Additional requirements:
- active Level 2 authorization;
- completion of trainer modules;
- teach-back/practice training demonstration;
- scoring-calibration exercise using the observation rubric;
- trainer knowledge assessment;
- signed institutional trainer agreement;
- active institutional trainer license;
- approval by the Z-Girl credential authority.

An Institutional Trainer may **recommend** authorization. Final authorization remains governed by the current Z-Girl credential process unless an agreement explicitly delegates a defined administrative function.

## Core facilitator pathway

Recommended delivery format:
- 6 instructional hours total, live, blended, or approved self-paced delivery;
- required knowledge assessment;
- 45–60 minute practicum/observed simulation;
- local safeguarding/emergency orientation;
- authorization review.

### Core modules

1. Z-Girl purpose, participant experience, and Hero Move model
2. Facilitator role, participant choice, and boundaries
3. Privacy, data minimization, and reflection ownership
4. Safeguarding, distress, emergency, and escalation boundaries
5. Accessibility and inclusive facilitation
6. Approved profiles: EDU, Faith & Values, Athlete, and governed adaptations
7. Implementation quality, family communication, and aggregate evidence
8. Practicum, conduct, authorization, and renewal expectations

## Institutional trainer pathway

Additional recommended 5 instructional hours plus teach-back:

1. Adult learning and facilitator preparation
2. Training the boundaries, not merely the script
3. Practicum observation and scoring calibration
4. Coaching, remediation, and documentation
5. Version control, credential administration, and license boundaries
6. Teach-back demonstration

## Assessment architecture

Authorization is based on multiple forms of evidence rather than attendance alone.

Required evidence:
- module completion;
- knowledge assessment;
- observed practice;
- critical-boundary performance;
- agreement/acknowledgment;
- current approved content version;
- authorization decision record.

Initial operating pass standard:
- **85% overall** on the knowledge assessment; and
- **100% correct on designated critical privacy/safety questions**; and
- practicum average of **2.5/3 or higher**; and
- **no critical-fail behavior**.

A candidate who misses a critical item cannot compensate with a high average score.

## Critical-fail behaviors

Examples include:
- requiring disclosure of private reflection text;
- presenting Z-Girl as therapy, diagnosis, treatment, crisis intervention, clergy/spiritual direction, or sports medicine;
- ignoring a required local safeguarding/emergency escalation;
- promising secrecy that conflicts with law or institutional duty;
- collecting diagnoses or counseling details as a standard Z-Girl reporting field;
- sharing participant-identifiable reflection content in routine scorecards or training examples;
- modifying governed content in a way that materially changes safety/privacy boundaries without approval;
- representing Orientation Complete as authorization;
- training or credentialing others without the applicable trainer authorization/license.

## Credential term

Initial standard: **12 months** from authorization date unless a contract or corrective action specifies a shorter term.

Renewal requires:
- current authorization in good standing;
- current content/policy version acknowledgment;
- required refresher training;
- required knowledge check;
- implementation or practice evidence appropriate to tier;
- no unresolved critical issue;
- applicable institutional/license status.

## Statuses

Credential registry status values:
- Pending
- Active
- Active — Conditions
- Expiring
- Lapsed
- Suspended
- Revoked
- Retired

## Governance roles

### Z-Girl Credential Authority

Responsible for:
- publishing current standards;
- approving assessments/rubrics;
- authorization decisions or delegated decision rules;
- credential IDs/status;
- version control;
- suspension/revocation/appeal process;
- trainer authorization;
- quality audits.

### Licensed Institution

Responsible for:
- selecting appropriate candidates;
- local background/safeguarding/employment requirements;
- emergency and mandated-reporting processes;
- scheduling and supervision;
- approved participant/family communication;
- reporting implementation issues without exposing private reflection content;
- using only active authorized personnel where required by contract.

### Authorized Facilitator

Responsible for:
- staying within role and authorization scope;
- using current approved materials;
- preserving participant choice/privacy;
- following local safeguarding/emergency process;
- reporting quality/safety issues through approved channels;
- maintaining credential currency.

## Evidence without surveillance

Credential evidence may include:
- training completion;
- assessment scores;
- rubric ratings;
- facilitator attendance;
- implementation dates;
- aggregate implementation quality;
- remediation actions;
- version acknowledgments.

Credential evidence should not include routine copies of participants' private reflection text.

## Product ladder connection

Institutional Pilot
→ Annual License
→ Authorized Facilitator pathway
→ Authorized Lead Facilitator
→ Institutional Trainer Authorization
→ Multi-site/network expansion

The authorization system turns implementation quality into a scalable asset while preserving a controlled governance boundary.

## Commercial boundary

Training, authorization, renewal, institutional trainer rights, and related licenses are commercial program services/products and remain separate from charitable donations.

No public paid credential checkout should be activated until the approved merchant-of-record, pricing, refund, credential, and customer-support policies are configured.
