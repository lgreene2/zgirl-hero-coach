# Z-Girl v3.11 — Pilot Evidence & Case-Study Standard

## Evidence classes

Each institutional evidence record must identify both **provenance** and **claim type**.

Claim types:
- observed
- system analytic
- administrative fact
- participant reported
- facilitator reported
- administrator reported

Never rewrite a reported perception as an observed outcome.

## Quantitative evidence

Use aggregate counts that can be defended from the named source: invited, activated, active, activities started/completed, reflection-session count, support requests and accessibility issues.

The system rejects impossible aggregate relationships such as activated exceeding invited or invited exceeding approved capacity.

Private reflection content is not an institutional metric.

## Qualitative evidence

Acceptable examples include implementation friction, administrator feedback, facilitator observations, aggregate participant feedback, configuration changes, accessibility observations, support patterns, renewal signals and expansion signals.

Do not store participant case narratives, diagnosis/treatment information, counseling notes, safeguarding narratives, clinical data, clergy/spiritual-direction data or sports-medicine data in the pilot evidence layer.

## Publication permissions

Testimonial, case-study, institutional-reference and funder-evidence permissions are tracked separately. A status of `granted` requires an actual permission/approval reference.

Participation in a pilot does not imply permission for public use.

## Case-study readiness

A case study may be marked ready only when:
- the pilot has completed a real implementation cycle;
- evidence provenance is documented;
- material claims can be supported;
- the relevant public-use permissions are granted;
- the institution has reviewed/approved the intended attribution where required;
- no participant private-reflection information is exposed.

## Reuse stack

A completed evidence base may be repurposed into:
- implementation summary
- executive outcome brief
- institutional case study
- approved testimonial record
- institutional reference record
- sales proof
- grant/funder evidence where appropriate
- renewal proposal
- expansion proposal
- facilitator-training source material

Every derivative asset must preserve the original evidence provenance and permission boundary.
