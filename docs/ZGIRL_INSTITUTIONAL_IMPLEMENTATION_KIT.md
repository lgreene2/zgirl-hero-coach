# Z-Girl Hero Within — Institutional Onboarding & Implementation Kit

Version: v2.6 working package

Purpose: provide one reusable operating system for moving an approved Z-Girl institutional engagement from fit review through pilot delivery, aggregate learning, closeout, renewal, licensing, and expansion.

## Who this kit serves

This kit is designed for:

- schools and youth programs using Z-Girl EDU;
- congregations and faith communities using approved Faith & Values profiles;
- athlete teams and leagues using Athlete Edition;
- approved specialized design partners;
- Z-Girl implementation leads, facilitators, coaches, administrators, and institutional decision-makers.

## Core operating principle

**The institution supports the reflection process. The reflection belongs to the participant.**

The implementation system is designed to help organizations deliver a structured reflection experience without converting private reflection text into routine institutional reporting data.

## Non-negotiable implementation boundaries

Every institutional implementation should preserve these boundaries unless a separately approved, legally reviewed agreement explicitly changes a requirement:

1. Private participant reflection text is not routine institutional reporting data.
2. Institutions do not receive a dashboard of private youth, student, athlete, or participant reflections.
3. Z-Girl is not therapy, medical care, clinical assessment, emergency support, clergy, spiritual direction, or sports medicine.
4. Facilitators do not diagnose, score mental health conditions, or pressure participants to disclose private reflections.
5. Participants retain pause, skip, stop, and trusted-person pathways.
6. Only approved aggregate implementation and experience measures are used for routine pilot learning.
7. Family-facing communication is used when appropriate to the setting, age group, law, agreement, and institutional policy.
8. Specialized content profiles are governed and versioned rather than silently changing the core Z-Girl system.
9. Commercial product and license payments remain separate from charitable donations.
10. Institutions remain responsible for their own safeguarding, emergency, mandated-reporting, consent/permission, accessibility, technology, records-management, and legal compliance obligations.

## The implementation lifecycle

### Stage 1 — Fit & Readiness

Use `ZGIRL_INSTITUTIONAL_READINESS_ASSESSMENT.md` before scheduling launch.

Outcome: **Ready / Ready with Conditions / Not Yet Ready**.

### Stage 2 — Implementation Planning

Use `ZGIRL_INSTITUTIONAL_IMPLEMENTATION_CALENDAR.md` to assign owners, dates, communications, orientation, sessions, measurement windows, and closeout milestones.

Outcome: one agreed implementation calendar.

### Stage 3 — Facilitator Orientation

Use `ZGIRL_FACILITATOR_ORIENTATION_GUIDE.md` before any facilitator, coach, educator, ministry leader, or program staff member leads participants through Z-Girl.

Outcome: documented facilitator readiness and boundary acknowledgment.

### Stage 4 — Family / Participant Communication

Use `ZGIRL_FAMILY_NOTICE_TEMPLATE.md` as a customizable starting point for family-facing communication where appropriate.

Outcome: clear explanation of purpose, participation expectations, privacy boundary, support route, and institutional contact.

### Stage 5 — Pilot Delivery & Aggregate Learning

Use `ZGIRL_AGGREGATE_PILOT_SCORECARD.md` to capture implementation measures without collecting private reflection text.

Outcome: evidence about delivery quality, usefulness, accessibility, participation, and next-step readiness.

### Stage 6 — Closeout

Use `ZGIRL_PILOT_CLOSEOUT_REPORT_TEMPLATE.md` to summarize what happened, what was learned, what should change, and what decision is recommended.

Outcome: one decision-ready closeout report.

### Stage 7 — Renewal / Expansion Decision

Use `ZGIRL_RENEWAL_EXPANSION_DECISION_PACKAGE.md` to decide whether to stop, revise, repeat, renew, license, expand, or prepare for Train-the-Trainer.

Outcome: documented next-step decision with scope, conditions, and owner.

## Recommended document-control fields

Each active implementation should track:

- organization name;
- Z-Girl pathway/profile;
- implementation owner;
- executive sponsor/decision-maker;
- facilitator(s);
- audience and approximate participant count;
- location/site(s);
- implementation dates;
- version of Z-Girl materials used;
- accessibility/support conditions;
- safeguarding escalation route;
- family communication requirement;
- approved aggregate measures;
- closeout date;
- renewal/expansion decision date.

## Minimum implementation record

The institutional record should document delivery and governance, not private participant reflection content. At minimum retain, according to the governing agreement and applicable policy:

- readiness decision;
- implementation calendar;
- facilitator orientation completion;
- approved family/participant communication;
- aggregate scorecard;
- material implementation issues and corrective actions;
- closeout report;
- renewal/expansion decision.

Do **not** place private reflection text into these records.

## Suggested ownership model

**Z-Girl implementation lead** — protects product fidelity, approved profile, facilitator readiness, scorecard structure, and closeout process.

**Institutional implementation owner** — coordinates local staffing, schedule, participant access, facilities/technology, communications, policy alignment, safeguarding routes, and local approvals.

**Facilitator / coach / educator** — creates a respectful environment, follows approved implementation guidance, preserves participant choice, and escalates concerns through institutional procedures rather than attempting clinical intervention.

**Executive sponsor / decision-maker** — approves scope, receives aggregate closeout information, and makes renewal or expansion decisions.

## Completion standard

An institutional pilot is considered operationally complete when:

- the planned implementation cycle has ended;
- aggregate measures have been compiled;
- material implementation issues have been documented;
- private reflection text has not been used as routine institutional reporting data;
- a closeout conversation has occurred;
- a next-step decision has been recorded.
