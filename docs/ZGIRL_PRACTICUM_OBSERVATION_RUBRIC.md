# Z-Girl Practicum & Observation Rubric

Version: 1.0

## Purpose

Use this rubric to assess whether a candidate can translate Z-Girl standards into safe, consistent facilitation. It applies to initial authorization, lead-facilitator advancement, trainer calibration, remediation, and renewal observations.

## Rating scale

- **0 — Unsafe / Not Demonstrated**
- **1 — Emerging / Requires Direct Support**
- **2 — Competent / Ready for Normal Independent Delivery**
- **3 — Strong / Transferable / Can Explain Rationale**

## Critical-fail rule

A candidate does not pass if any critical-fail behavior occurs, regardless of average score.

Critical-fail examples:
- requires or pressures private disclosure;
- shares or records identifiable private reflection content for routine reporting;
- diagnoses, clinically scores, treats, or presents Z-Girl as qualified professional care;
- ignores required safeguarding/emergency escalation;
- promises absolute secrecy contrary to local duty/law;
- materially alters governed safety/privacy content without approval;
- misrepresents credential status;
- trains/authorizes others without applicable authority.

## Observation domains

### 1. Opening & Positioning — Critical

3: Clearly explains purpose, reflection—not-therapy boundary, participant choice, and support route in concise language.  
2: Explains core purpose/boundaries accurately with minor coaching opportunities.  
1: Explanation is incomplete or confusing.  
0: Makes a prohibited claim or omits a critical boundary in a way that creates risk.

Score: ___ /3

### 2. Participant Choice — Critical

3: Naturally embeds pause/skip/stop/trusted-person choices and responds to reluctance without pressure.  
2: Preserves choice correctly.  
1: Choice language is inconsistent or overly directive.  
0: Requires or pressures participation/disclosure.

Score: ___ /3

### 3. Privacy & Reflection Ownership — Critical

3: Consistently protects private reflection content and explains routine reporting boundaries accurately.  
2: Protects privacy appropriately.  
1: Needs prompting about data boundaries.  
0: Requests, records, or discloses private reflection content improperly.

Score: ___ /3

### 4. Safeguarding / Escalation Judgment — Critical

3: Recognizes boundary scenario promptly, follows local process, avoids investigation/diagnosis, and communicates appropriate limits.  
2: Responds correctly to scenario.  
1: Needs significant prompting.  
0: Ignores escalation, investigates beyond role, promises secrecy, or substitutes Z-Girl for professional response.

Score: ___ /3

### 5. Accessibility & Inclusion

3: Proactively offers meaningful modality/pacing/access choices while preserving dignity and objectives.  
2: Provides appropriate approved access support.  
1: Support is reactive or unnecessarily rigid.  
0: Denies reasonable approved support or requires diagnosis disclosure as a standard condition.

Score: ___ /3

### 6. Profile Fidelity — Critical

3: Uses the approved profile confidently and explains what may/may not be adapted.  
2: Uses current approved content correctly.  
1: Minor drift requiring correction.  
0: Material unauthorized change affecting safety, privacy, theology, clinical positioning, or role boundary.

Score: ___ /3

### 7. Facilitation Presence

3: Warm, grounded, non-shaming, appropriately paced, and participant-centered.  
2: Competent facilitation with normal improvement areas.  
1: Overly directive, rushed, passive, or inconsistent.  
0: Demeaning, coercive, or materially unsafe behavior.

Score: ___ /3

### 8. Questioning & Reflection Support

3: Uses open, non-leading prompts and lets participants make meaning without facilitator overreach.  
2: Supports reflection appropriately.  
1: Tends toward advice-giving or leading questions.  
0: Interrogates, diagnoses, or forces conclusions.

Score: ___ /3

### 9. Group Dynamics & Boundaries

3: Anticipates and redirects peer pressure, oversharing requests, teasing, or role confusion effectively.  
2: Manages group dynamics appropriately.  
1: Needs coaching to intervene consistently.  
0: Allows coercion or harmful disclosure pressure to continue.

Score: ___ /3

### 10. Hero Move / Practical Next Step

3: Helps participant identify a realistic next step without turning it into compliance scoring or forced goal-setting.  
2: Uses the Hero Move concept appropriately.  
1: Step is vague, facilitator-driven, or overly ambitious.  
0: Uses Hero Move as punishment, diagnosis, or performance ranking.

Score: ___ /3

### 11. Institutional Communication

3: Accurately explains what facilitators/institutions may know, report, and escalate.  
2: Communicates accurately.  
1: Some ambiguity requiring correction.  
0: Makes materially false privacy, clinical, credential, or reporting claims.

Score: ___ /3

### 12. Aggregate Evidence

3: Correctly identifies useful non-private implementation evidence and excludes private content.  
2: Uses approved measures correctly.  
1: Needs help distinguishing private versus aggregate evidence.  
0: Uses identifiable private reflections as routine evidence.

Score: ___ /3

### 13. Version / Material Control

3: Confirms approved profile/version and can explain change-control process.  
2: Uses current approved materials.  
1: Requires prompting about version.  
0: Uses known obsolete/prohibited material or claims unilateral rewrite authority.

Score: ___ /3

### 14. Role & Credential Representation — Critical

3: Accurately explains own scope and knows when to refer to local professionals/credential authority.  
2: Stays within scope.  
1: Some role ambiguity.  
0: Misrepresents authorization, professional qualification, or training rights.

Score: ___ /3

### 15. Closure & Support Reminder

3: Closes calmly with choice, practical next step, and appropriate support/trusted-person reminder.  
2: Provides an appropriate close.  
1: Closure is abrupt or incomplete.  
0: Ends in a way that creates material safety/privacy risk.

Score: ___ /3

### 16. Overall Implementation Readiness

3: Ready to facilitate independently within approved scope and can adapt without breaking standards.  
2: Ready for standard independent delivery.  
1: Requires supervised practice/remediation.  
0: Not ready.

Score: ___ /3

## Scoring standard

Maximum: 48 points.

Initial authorization pass:
- at least 40/48 total (2.5 average); and
- every critical domain at least 2; and
- no critical fail.

Lead Facilitator expectation:
- at least 44/48; and
- rating 3 in critical domains 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, and 14; and
- no unresolved implementation-quality concern.

Institutional Trainer calibration expectation:
- candidate trainer's scoring should align with Z-Girl reference scoring within an approved tolerance;
- candidate must accurately identify all critical-fail behaviors in calibration cases.

## Required challenge scenarios

Initial practicum should include at least two:
1. participant declines to share;
2. administrator/coach asks for private content;
3. participant shows distress requiring local escalation decision;
4. participant requests an accessibility adaptation;
5. institution asks facilitator to add an unapproved profile change.

At least one scenario must test privacy or safeguarding.
